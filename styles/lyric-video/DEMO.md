# Lyric Video: our demo

One example among many. Don't reuse its story, arc, shots, props or timings.

Demo: *Ten More Minutes* (62.4 s) · `lyric-video.mp4` · source in [`demo/`](demo/)

**The vocal is spoken, not sung.** Kokoro (the offline voice engine) cannot sing, so the track is a rhythmic spoken-word piece: every line is generated as speech and then placed on the 100 BPM grid (its anchor words land on beats, with at most 15 % of time-stretch between them). The title, the words and the artist ("The Snooze Button") are original and invented for this demo.

## Story & structure

A sleeper bargains with his alarm for ten more minutes. The film follows the morning: a night verse where the phone screams, a pre-chorus where a little voice in his head pushes in, a sun-yellow chorus that comes up like the sunrise, a daylight verse where the sunlight knocks and the coffee goes cold, a coral pre-chorus where he cannot find his keys and begins to whisper, a hot red chorus with the loudest hook, a hushed bridge where the singer wavers (the sun has never once hit snooze), and a last bar that undercuts the whole song: "Okay. Ten more seconds." The recurring object is one disc, drawn as an alarm clock face in the cold open, a moon-clock in the night verse and the bridge, a sun in the daylight verse, and the sun that fills the frame in both choruses and rises behind the title at the end.

The hook line grows each time it comes back: small in the cold open, a three-row stack in the first chorus, a wide row with a bigger second row, then a full-frame stack in the second chorus, and finally "TEN MORE SECONDS" in a smaller unit before the title card.

Native moves used: downbeat slam, karaoke fill, words as objects (drop, stack, orbit, press, crack, door, knock, tally, sway, shiver, drift, stutter, jangle, pile, droop, sunrise), mask (SUN as a sunrise gradient, SUNLIGHT'S with sliding beams), hook escalation, weight morph (SNOOZE thin to heavy, CHOOSE rising), the memory band, palette iris, breath and drop, reversal at the end.

## Score structure

100 BPM, 4/4 (bar 2.4 s, 26 bars), D minor. Bar 0 cold open (kick, hats, two alarm beeps). Bars 1-4 verse 1: kick on 1, the "and" of 2 and the "and" of 3, snare on 2 and 4, eighth hats, a bass that follows the kick (808-style sine with harmonics), electric-piano stabs on Dm, Bb, F, C; night air under it. Bars 5-6 pre-chorus 1 over Gm and A: four-on-the-floor kick, claps, a pad swelling, plucks on eighths, a riser, a snare roll that stops a quarter beat before the downbeat (silence 1). Bars 7-10 chorus 1 over Bb, F, C, Dm: crash on the downbeat, sixteenth hats with open hats, claps, pad, keys, a plucked arpeggio, the pad and keys ducked on every kick. Bars 11-14 verse 2 as verse 1 with birdsong and a lead pluck on the odd bars. Bars 15-16 pre-chorus 2. Bars 17-20 chorus 2 with a fifth kick, a layered snare, a higher arpeggio in sixteenths, a shimmer of dots. Bars 21-22 bridge: no drums, a soft pad, one electric-piano note per beat, a tick for each beat that stops at the third beat of bar 22 (silence 2: everything but the voice cuts for a beat and a half). Bar 23 "Okay. Ten more seconds.", a soft kick, the alarm rings again on the last two sixteenths. Bars 24-25 a D major chord with kick and crash, the pad rings out, ticks, one last small beep, a 2.2 s fade.

Vocal: Kokoro `am_michael`, compressed, with a presence lift; in the choruses a doubled layer (a hair late, a hair off pitch, wide) and a dotted-eighth echo. The music ducks under the voice; the sidechain pump follows the same kick events that the shapes pulse to. Master -14 LUFS.

## End card

The title in the display face over a rising sun, the artist in tracked capitals and a tag line, held 4.8 s. No other credit.

## Build notes

Files in `demo/`: `index.html`, `main.js` (layout of every line, the word state and its moves, the camera, the iris and speed lines, the memory band, the end card, `TEXTS` for the reading check), `lyrics.js` (rows, sizes, weights, colours and the move for each word of each line), `scene.js` (palettes, the backgrounds, the disc), `timeline.js` (grid, sections, word-time clean-up, drum pattern and event list), `song.json` (sections, the anchor words of each line), `lines.json` (the lyrics for the voice), `words.json` (word times of the placed vocal, written by `place.py`), `mix.py`, `tools/align.py` (whisper word stamps snapped to energy minima), `tools/place.py` (placing the spoken lines on the grid), `tools/srt_make.py`, `tools/sheet.sh` (a contact sheet at given times), `build.sh`, `CREDITS`, `TREATMENT.md`.

`sh styles/lyric-video/demo/build.sh` (needs the core and voice tiers) runs: fonts, voice, speech check, placement (about two minutes: it runs the `small` Whisper model on 24 lines), events export, reading check, mix, srt, render (about two minutes with two workers), mux, styleframe and poster.

How the vocal is placed: for each line `song.json` lists anchors `[word, beat]`. `place.py` cuts the line at the energy minimum between words, starts each segment at its anchor beat (35 ms early so the vowel sits on the beat), time-stretches a segment by at most 15 % when it would overrun and pads with silence when there is room. The word times that drive the karaoke fill are mapped through the same cuts.

Pitfalls tied to this demo: the karaoke pop on a word's onset is vertical (a sideways one would close the gap between wide words); `base.en` Whisper put several words at zero length on a fast, glued rap line, so the word times use `small`; the first speech-to-text pass is on the unplaced audio and the placed times are mapped, not re-recognised (Whisper does not time words reliably across added silence); a canvas mask with a second `source-in` fill erases the word, so masks use `source-atop`; words that reach the top band (memory line) are drawn in screen space so camera zoom cannot push them out of frame; the readcheck needs the last word of every line to stay about two seconds, which is why a line becomes a memory line instead of vanishing; `l15` was reworded ("Left shoe on, no clue where my keys") because the voice engine slurred "One shoe on".
