# Facts used in "The Birth of a Phone"

Every date, name and number that appears on screen or in the narration, with its source. The film is an unofficial, educational film about real historical events and products; it uses no logos, official artwork or photographs (everything is drawn in code, the phone is a generic glass slab).

| Claim in the film | Source | Wording checked |
|---|---|---|
| About 40,000 years ago people left hand stencils on cave walls (plate "约4万年前", narration "大约四万年前") | Aubert et al., "Pleistocene cave art from Sulawesi, Indonesia", Nature 514, 223-227 (2014): https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25297435/ | "The earliest dated image from Maros, with a minimum age of 39.9 kyr, is now the oldest known hand stencil in the world." |
| The earliest writing is on clay tablets from Mesopotamia, about 3300 BC (plate "约前3300年", narration "五千多年前") | Smarthistory, "Cuneiform, an introduction": https://smarthistory.org/cuneiform | "Writing was invented in Mesopotamia in about 3300 B.C., perhaps in the city of Uruk, where the earliest inscribed clay tablets have been found" |
| The Diamond Sutra, printed from woodblocks in 868, is the world's earliest dated printed book (plate "868年", narration "现存最早标有日期的印刷书") | Oxford (Cabinet), "The world's earliest dated printed book: The Diamond Sutra, 868 CE": https://www.cabinet.ox.ac.uk/worlds-earliest-dated-printed-book-diamond-sutra-868-ce | Colophon: "the 15th of the 4th moon of the 9th year of Xiantong [11 May 868]" |
| The Gutenberg Bible was printed c. 1455 with movable metal type (plate "约1455年", narration "约1455年，古腾堡用活字印出《圣经》") | Same Oxford page (it states the Gutenberg Bible date): https://www.cabinet.ox.ac.uk/worlds-earliest-dated-printed-book-diamond-sutra-868-ce | "the Gutenberg Bible, printed in Latin in Germany c.1455 ... the world's earliest book printed with movable metal type" |
| The first long-distance telegraph message was sent from Washington to Baltimore on 24 May 1844 (plate "1844年", narration "莫尔斯从华盛顿发出电报……传到了巴尔的摩") | Library of Congress, "What Hath God Wrought?" (Today in History, May 24): https://www.loc.gov/item/today-in-history/may-24/ ; paper tape: https://www.loc.gov/resource/mmorse.071009 | the message "What hath God wrought" went from the Capitol in Washington to Baltimore on May 24, 1844 (the film shows only the route, not the words) |
| Bell's telephone patent was issued in 1876 (plate "1876年", narration "贝尔拿到电话专利") | US Patent 174,465, "Improvement in Telegraphy", issued 7 March 1876: https://patents.google.com/patent/US174465A/en | "No. 174,465 ... Patented March 7, 1876" |
| The first handheld mobile phone call was made in 1973 by Martin Cooper of Motorola on a New York street (plate "1973年", narration "库珀在纽约街头……打出第一通手持移动电话") | AARP summary of the 3 April 1973 call: https://www.aarp.org/politics-society/history/info-2018/first-cell-phone-call.html | "the first handheld cellular phone call was made on April 3, 1973, by Motorola engineer Martin Cooper from Sixth Avenue in New York" (the call went to Joel Engel at Bell Labs) |
| The first SMS was sent on 3 December 1992 and read "Merry Christmas" (plate "1992年", narration "1992年12月3日，第一条短信发出，只有四个字：圣诞快乐"; the pixel text box shows "MERRY CHRISTMAS") | History.com, "First SMS text message sent": https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/december-3/first-sms-text-message-sent ; Neil Papworth: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Papworth | "On December 3, 1992, Neil Papworth sent the world's first text message ... 'Merry Christmas'" (the Chinese rendering has four characters) |
| iPhone was introduced on 9 January 2007 (plate "2007年", narration "第一代iPhone发布") | Apple Newsroom, "Apple Reinvents the Phone with iPhone": https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2007/01/09Apple-Reinvents-the-Phone-with-iPhone/ | "MACWORLD SAN FRANCISCO, January 9, 2007 - Apple today introduced iPhone" |

Not facts, by design: the courier, the message ("我来过", "I was here"), the idea that a hand stencil is a message, and the closing line that the phone's screen holds the whole scroll are the film's own story.
