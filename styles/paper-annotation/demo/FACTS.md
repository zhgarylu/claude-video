# Facts: how to read a paper

The paper in this demo is **fictional**. Every number on its pages (58 %, 0.7 points, the table, the bars) was invented and made to agree with itself:

| Table 1 (invented) | Latency full / ours (s) | F1 full / ours |
|---|---|---|
| Bench A | 10.0 / 4.2 (-58.0 %) | 71.2 / 70.4 (-0.8) |
| Bench B | 8.0 / 3.5 (-56.3 %) | 68.5 / 67.9 (-0.6) |
| Bench C | 14.0 / 5.6 (-60.0 %) | 74.1 / 73.4 (-0.7) |
| Average | 10.67 / 4.43 (-58 %) | 71.27 / 70.57 (-0.7) |

Figure 2 shows the averages (10.7 vs 4.4 s; 71.3 vs 70.6 F1). "0.7 points" means percentage points of F1.

The reading advice is general, not a claim about any real paper: check what the authors compare against (one baseline is weaker evidence than several), whether the numbers are self-reported, and where the method is said to fail (the limitations section). "Skim-then-Read" is not a real method named in the literature as far as this demo knows; any resemblance to a real paper is accidental.
